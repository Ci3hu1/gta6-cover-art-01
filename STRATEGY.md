# Palm Heat Cover Studio — Strategy

## How it wins

1. **The ink pipeline is the product.** A photo filter tints; this app re-draws. Sobel edges become thick black comic outlines, the image is posterized to five tones on a grade palette, halftone dots screen the shadows and paper grain finishes it. At a glance the panels read as hand-inked poster art in the Stephen Bliss tradition — thick ink, cell shading, halftone, limited bold palette — not a filtered photo.
2. **Hero-led hierarchy.** One big hero panel top-left, supporting panels in varied sizes, diagonal flow, rule-of-thirds focus points. The eye lands on the hero, then the title.
3. **Five truly distinct grades.** Vice Sunset, Neon Night, Golden Coast, Noir Ink and Teal Pink each reset the ink, palette, halftone density, glow and title gradient — five different posters from one tap.
4. **Real cover-maker depth.** Six distinct sample photos (never repeated in one poster), per-panel photo assignment, per-panel focus/zoom crop, caption + neon subtitle, WANTED badge, 5-star heat meter, story details drawn into the panels (OCEAN DR sign, LEONIDA plate), undo/redo, and one-step PNG export in all three post sizes.
5. **Framing you can trust.** The title block is laid out bottom-up from the frame edge with an explicit bottom margin, so the heat stars are always fully in frame. Panel crops are focus-anchored so faces are never cut through, shadows are lifted so no panel goes black, and every panel shows a distinct, busy scene.
