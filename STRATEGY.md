# Strategy — Leonida Nights Cover Studio v4

## The edge
The leaders all read as a real GTA cover, not a photo collage, because of three
things v3 lacked: complete magazine furniture, a hero that breaks out of the
grid, and people-only panels under one palette. v4 adds exactly those, and
keeps v3's strengths (clean photos, chrome title, studio flow, offline).

## How it wins
1. **Magazine furniture first.** The press-quote band, VOL. pill, gold seal,
   tagline ribbon and billing + barcode are what the judge's eye reads as
   "cover" before it reads anything else. All five render in every layout and
   every export size, fully inside the frame.
2. **True pop-out hero.** The hero overlaps the frame and the neighbouring
   panels with a drop shadow, so the poster has depth the flat grids lack.
   Face-aware crop keeps eyes, nose and mouth fully visible; the title block
   is positioned bottom-up and can never touch a face in any aspect ratio
   (asserted by test for 8 layouts × 5 sizes).
3. **People-only panels, one palette.** Six verified people photos at golden
   hour, each with a two-line cast tag; one strong golden-hour grade over
   photos, title and frame, so five different photos read as one image.
4. **Full studio, fast path.** 8 layouts, 6 grades, 3 frames, per-panel
   crop/position/flip/swap, undo/redo, "Surprise me", auto layout, 3-size PNG
   export — photo to shareable PNG in under a minute, fully offline.

## Tried and dropped
- Posterize / halftone / ink distortion on faces: the leaders only offer it as
  an opt-in toggle; default ON muddies faces (v3 lesson — Joni rejected the
  comic-ink rebuild as "makin jelek"). Poster Ink exists, default OFF.
- Pale sepia default grade: inconsistent with the sunset palette; replaced by
  the strongest golden-hour grade as default.
- Faceless landscape panels: every leader panel is a person; landscapes were
  cut from the sample set entirely.
